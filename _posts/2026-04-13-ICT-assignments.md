If someone had told me at the start of my first semester that I would 
be making Word documents, PowerPoint presentations, and completing 
assignment after assignment on computer basics — I would have probably 
said "that sounds easy." But nothing about first semester was easy. 
And AICT assignments were no exception.

AICT — Introduction to Computer and Information Technology — sounds 
simple on paper. Basics of computers, MS Word, PowerPoint presentations. 
But when you are a fresh student, still adjusting to university life, 
hostel life, new subjects, new people, and a completely new environment 
— even the "simple" things feel heavy. Every assignment felt like a 
mountain when you already had three other subjects demanding your 
attention at the same time.

The assignments came one after another. Word documents that needed 
proper formatting, headings, tables, and layouts. PowerPoint 
presentations that needed design, content, and flow. Each one had 
its own requirements, its own deadline, and its own challenges. There 
were nights where I sat in my hostel room, laptop open, trying to 
figure out how to format something correctly or why my presentation 
was not looking the way I wanted it to look.

All of them were tough in their own way. Word felt frustrating when 
formatting would not cooperate. PowerPoint felt overwhelming when 
you had to make something that actually looked presentable. There 
was no single "hardest" assignment — they were all hard in different 
ways at different times. The difficulty was not just the work itself 
but the pressure of doing it all while managing everything else 
university life throws at you.

But there is something deeply satisfying about finishing something 
that felt impossible. When I submitted that last assignment, when 
everything was done and handed in — the relief was indescribable. 
That feeling of "I did it" after days of stress and struggle is 
something no one can take away from you. It sits in your chest like 
a quiet victory.

What AICT truly gave me was a solid foundation in computer basics. 
Before university, I used a computer — but I did not really understand 
it. Through these assignments, I learned how to use tools properly, 
how to present information clearly, and how to work under pressure 
and deadlines. These are skills that sound small but matter enormously 
in the long run.

Every assignment I completed made me a little more confident. A little 
more capable. And that confidence — built through struggle, late nights, 
and the relief of submission — is something I carry into every challenge 
that comes after this.
