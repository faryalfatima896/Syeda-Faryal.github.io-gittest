Nobody tells you how different university exams are from school exams. 
Nobody warns you that six subjects hitting you all at once, in a 
curriculum you are still trying to understand, feels like standing 
in the middle of a storm with no umbrella. My first midterm season 
at UET Lahore Faisalabad Campus was exactly that — a storm. And I 
walked into it completely unprepared.

Six subjects. Six papers. And a university curriculum that was nothing 
like anything I had experienced before. Back in school, I knew the 
system. I knew what to expect, how to prepare, what kind of questions 
would come. University was a completely different world. The syllabus 
was vast, the concepts were deeper, and the expectations were higher. 
I remember sitting with my books thinking — where do I even begin?

Sleep became a luxury I could not afford. Nights blurred into mornings. 
I would sit in my hostel room, notes spread everywhere, trying to cram 
weeks worth of content into a few desperate hours. Python code, 
database concepts, formulas, definitions — everything jumbled together 
in my head. There were moments of pure panic, moments where I just 
stared at the wall wondering how anyone gets through this.

Python was one of the subjects that stressed me out the most during 
midterms. We had just started getting comfortable with the basics and 
suddenly we had to reproduce logic, write functions, and solve problems 
under exam pressure. The difference between understanding something in 
class and performing under exam conditions is enormous. My hands would 
shake typing code I had written a hundred times before.

The stress was real. The exhaustion was real. The anxiety before each 
paper was real. Walking into that exam hall with sleepy eyes and a 
racing heart is an experience every university student knows — but 
living it for the first time hits differently. There were both good 
moments and terrible moments. Papers that went better than expected 
and papers that left me walking out in silence.

But when it was all over — when the last paper was submitted and I 
walked out of that exam hall — I felt something shift inside me. I 
had survived. Not perfectly. Not without struggle. But I had made it 
through my first university midterm season and that meant something.

The biggest lesson midterms taught me was painfully simple — do your 
work on time. Do not wait for exam week to open your books. Do not 
let assignments pile up. Do not convince yourself you will understand 
it later. University moves fast and it waits for no one. Every day 
you delay is a night of sleep you will lose later. I learned that 
lesson the hard way — through six subjects, countless sleepless nights, 
and the kind of stress that only comes from leaving everything to the 
last minute.

If I could go back and tell myself one thing before that first midterm 
season — it would be this: start early, stay consistent, and trust the 
process. Because the curriculum makes sense eventually. The subjects 
connect eventually. But only if you give yourself enough time to let 
them.
