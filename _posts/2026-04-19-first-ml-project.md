When Dr. Bilal Ahmad assigned us our first Machine Learning project, 
I did not fully understand what I was getting into. We had a mall 
dataset — a real dataset containing different categories of products 
with various attributes and information. Looking at it for the first 
time, rows and columns of data stretching across the screen, I 
remember thinking — what exactly are we supposed to do with all of this?

The answer, as I would slowly discover over the next few weeks, was 
everything. We were supposed to clean it, understand it, visualize 
it, and ultimately train a machine learning model on it. For a first 
semester student who had only recently learned what a Python loop was, 
this felt like an enormous task.

The libraries came first. Pandas, Scikit-learn, Joblib, Matplotlib — 
four names that would become very familiar to me very quickly. Each 
one had a specific purpose. Pandas for handling and manipulating the 
data. Scikit-learn for building and training the machine learning 
model. Joblib for saving the trained model. Matplotlib for creating 
visualizations. Understanding what each library does, why we use it, 
and how it fits into the bigger picture was itself a major learning 
curve.

Dr. Bilal Ahmad guided us through everything step by step. He is an 
AI, ML and Deep Learning expert who believes in learning through doing 
— and this project was proof of that philosophy. He did not just give 
us the answer. He gave us the tools, the direction, and the 
understanding we needed to figure it out ourselves. Following his 
instructions carefully was the key to making progress when everything 
felt confusing.

The graphs were one of the most exciting parts of the project. Using 
Matplotlib, we created visualizations of our data — bar charts, 
scatter plots, distributions. Seeing data transform into a graph that 
actually told a story was a completely new experience. Suddenly the 
numbers meant something. The patterns became visible. I understood 
why Dr. Bilal Ahmad always emphasizes data visualization — because 
you cannot understand what you cannot see.

Understanding how everything connected was the real challenge. It was 
not just about writing code that ran without errors. It was about 
understanding why each step existed, what it was doing to the data, 
and how it contributed to the final model. That deeper understanding 
took time, patience, and a lot of re-reading and re-running code.

But when it all came together — when the model trained successfully, 
when the graphs rendered beautifully, when everything we had been 
working on finally made sense — the feeling was incredible. We had 
taken raw data, processed it, visualized it, and trained a machine 
learning model on it. As first semester students, that felt like 
something truly special.

Dr. Bilal Ahmad always reminds us that this is just the beginning. 
And he is right. But every big journey starts with a first step — 
and this project was mine into the world of real machine learning.
