Before university, the word "database" meant nothing to me. I had 
heard it somewhere — in a movie maybe, or a tech article I had 
scrolled past — but I had no real understanding of what it was or 
why it mattered. When Dr. Bilal Ahmad introduced Database Systems 
in our first semester at UET Lahore Faisalabad Campus, I was 
starting completely from zero.

The first thing Dr. Bilal Ahmad made us understand was the "why" 
behind databases. Why do we need them? Why not just store data in 
files? Why does structure matter? These questions seem simple but 
their answers open up an entire world of thinking. Data is 
everywhere. Every app you use, every website you visit, every 
system you interact with — there is a database behind it, quietly 
organizing and serving information. Understanding that changed how 
I looked at technology entirely.

The concept of tables, rows, and columns came first. Organizing 
data into structured tables where every piece of information has 
its place — this felt clean and logical. Then came relationships 
between tables. Primary keys, foreign keys, how one table connects 
to another — this is where database thinking really began to take 
shape. Data is not just stored, it is connected. And those 
connections tell the real story.

Dr. Bilal Ahmad always taught with real world scenarios. He never 
just gave us abstract definitions — he showed us how databases work 
in actual systems, actual applications, actual problems that real 
engineers solve every day. That approach made every concept feel 
relevant and purposeful rather than just something to memorize for 
an exam.

Lab sessions brought everything to life. Writing actual queries, 
working with real data, seeing the results of our commands appear 
on screen — this hands-on experience solidified everything we had 
learned in theory. There is a particular satisfaction in writing 
a database query and getting back exactly the data you asked for. 
It feels like the system is listening to you.

What this journey gave me was not just knowledge of databases — 
it gave me a new way of thinking about information. How to 
organize it. How to structure it. How to retrieve it efficiently. 
These are skills that go far beyond any single subject or exam.

My database journey is still just beginning. But every journey 
starts somewhere — and mine started in Dr. Bilal Ahmad's classroom, 
from absolute zero, one table at a time.
