When Dr. Bilal Ahmad first mentioned Machine Learning in our class, I 
remember thinking — what even is this? I had just started getting 
comfortable with Python, and suddenly we were talking about training 
models, datasets, and algorithms. It felt like someone handed me a 
bicycle and then immediately asked me to fly a plane.

But that is exactly how Dr. Bilal Ahmad works. He does not wait for you 
to feel ready. He believes in throwing you into the deep end because that 
is where real learning happens. And looking back now, I completely 
understand why.

The first thing he did was explain the concept of Machine Learning in 
detail — what it is, how it works, what features are, and why they 
matter. He broke down everything step by step. Features, labels, training 
data, test data — each concept was explained with real examples. But 
understanding the concept in theory and actually implementing it in code 
are two very different things. When I sat down to write my first ML code, 
I felt completely lost.

The libraries were the first challenge. Pandas, NumPy, Scikit-learn — 
names I had never heard before. Each library had its own functions, its 
own syntax, its own logic. Understanding what each one does and when to 
use it took a lot of time and patience. Dr. Bilal Ahmad provided us with 
detailed features and code to guide us, but even then, making sense of 
it all was not easy.

Our dataset was from a mall — it contained different categories of 
products with various attributes. Working with real data for the first 
time was eye-opening. Data is never clean. Data is never perfect. There 
were missing values, inconsistencies, and categories that did not make 
immediate sense. Learning to handle that messy reality was itself a huge 
lesson. Dr. Bilal Ahmad always emphasizes using datasets that have real 
value — he often pushes students toward medical datasets because of their 
precision and accuracy. Even our mall dataset taught us that same lesson 
— real world data requires real world thinking.

And then came the errors. So many errors. Errors I did not understand. 
Errors that made no sense. Errors that appeared even when I was sure my 
code was correct. There were moments of genuine frustration where I just 
sat staring at the screen not knowing what to do next. But Dr. Bilal 
Ahmad's instructions were always there to guide us. Following them 
carefully, one step at a time, things slowly started falling into place.

When the model finally ran without errors and produced output — that 
feeling was indescribable. We had trained a machine learning model. As 
first semester Computer Engineering students, we had done something that 
felt impossibly big. But as Dr. Bilal Ahmad reminded us — this was just 
the beginning. What felt like a massive achievement was actually just 
the first step of a very long and exciting journey.

That perspective is something I carry with me. No matter how big 
something feels right now, it is always just a starting point. And 
having a mentor like Dr. Bilal Ahmad — an AI, ML and Deep Learning 
expert who trains models on real world problems — makes that journey 
feel less scary and more exciting every single day.
